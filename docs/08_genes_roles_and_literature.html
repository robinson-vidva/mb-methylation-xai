8. Genes: names, cancer roles and literature¶

Page 8 of 9. previous: 7. Gene clusters and pathway flow | index | next: 9. Conclusions

The previous pages name genes by symbol. This page says what each gene is, whether it is a known cancer gene, what the literature says about it in medulloblastoma, and which signalling pathways it belongs to. It then reads every concordant change against that knowledge.

Sources, all public. Full names and the NCBI gene summary come from MyGene.info. Oncogene and tumour suppressor status comes from two curated lists: the OncoKB cancer gene list (gene type: oncogene, tumour suppressor, both, or neither) and the COSMIC Cancer Gene Census "role in cancer", read through the Open Targets Platform together with the COSMIC cancer hallmarks. Pathway membership is Reactome, also through Open Targets. Literature is a PubMed count of papers that mention the gene together with medulloblastoma in title or abstract, and the three most relevant of those papers.

The reading. A promoter that loses methylation switches a gene on; a promoter that gains methylation switches it off. Two combinations fit a causal story in cancer: an oncogene switched on by hypomethylation, and a tumour suppressor silenced by hypermethylation. The other two, an oncogene silenced or a tumour suppressor switched on, do not, and are more likely lineage markers that happen to carry a cancer label from another tissue. Most concordant genes have no cancer role in either list; for them the methylation change reports cell identity, not transformation.

5. Reading the biology, subgroup by subgroup¶

First, the overall count. Of 464 annotated genes, 46 carry a curated cancer role. Of the 325 concordant genes, 262 have never been mentioned together with medulloblastoma on PubMed. Both numbers say the same thing as page 7: the genes whose methylation defines a subgroup are mostly lineage genes, not cancer genes. That is expected for a tumour classified by cell of origin, and it is the reason the classifier works on FFPE blocks from another laboratory.

WNT. This is the subgroup where the reading has the most content, and the most instructive case is the one that looks wrong. Six tumour suppressors are switched on: RNF43, ZNRF3, APCDD1, ZFHX3, EPHA3 and SLFN11. RNF43 and ZNRF3 are E3 ubiquitin ligases that remove Frizzled receptors from the cell surface; APCDD1 is a membrane Wnt inhibitor. All three are transcriptional targets of beta-catenin, as are AXIN2, NKD1, NOTUM and DKK1 from page 7. They form the negative feedback loop of the pathway: when Wnt signalling fires, the cell induces its own brakes. In WNT medulloblastoma, CTNNB1 carries a mutation in the degradation motif, so beta-catenin is stable no matter what happens at the receptor. The brakes are induced and have nothing to act on. The methylation layer splits them in two: RNF43, ZNRF3, APCDD1 and SP5 are hypomethylated as well as expressed, while AXIN2, NKD1, NOTUM and DKK1 are expressed with no promoter change. "Tumour suppressor switched on" is here the signature of a pathway stuck on downstream of the point where the suppressor acts. The one tumour suppressor silenced, SFRP1, a secreted Wnt antagonist, is the classic promoter hypermethylation event of Wnt-driven cancers, and has ten medulloblastoma papers. The oncogenes switched on, SYK and STAT4, are immune signalling kinases and transcription factors with no medulloblastoma literature; they may reflect the immune and stromal content of WNT tumours rather than the tumour cell, and are leads at most.

SHH. Almost nothing with a cancer label is concordant. GLI2 and BOC are the pathway-level hits and carry the most literature (70 and 3 papers). APCDD1 silenced and NDRG1 switched on are the only labelled genes; NDRG1 is a hypoxia and differentiation gene. The SHH concordant list is dominated by genes of the desmoplastic stroma and of neuronal differentiation. The Hedgehog pathway itself is activated by mutation, as page 7 shows, and the methylation layer adds the context of the lineage, not the driver.

Group 3. IGF1 is the one oncogene switched on, with 15 medulloblastoma papers; insulin-like growth factor signalling is an established growth signal in Group 3 models and a candidate target. The rest of the labelled list is the photoreceptor program (RHO, GNAT1, GNGT1, GNGT2 in the signalling map), which is lineage identity, and GLI2 and OTX2 silenced or switched on as the mirror image of SHH and Group 4.

Group 4. No cancer-role gene fits the causal story. DDR2 and SCG5 are silenced, which reads as lineage again. The most cited concordant genes, OTX2, EYA1, EOMES and RBM24, are developmental transcription factors and RNA-binding proteins of the cerebellar lineage. Group 4 stays what the literature says it is: the subgroup defined by its cell of origin rather than by a driver pathway, with KDM6A, PRDM6 and SNCAIP as mutated or rearranged genes whose promoters do not change.

Caveats on the counts. PubMed counts use the gene symbol in title or abstract, so symbols that are also common abbreviations are inflated: FAP (familial adenomatous polyposis, itself linked to medulloblastoma), SAG (sagittal), SST, TF and RHO. Those counts should be read as upper bounds. Twenty-nine symbols come from the 2011 Illumina manifest and are no longer current (C1orf61, HIST1H3E, SEPP1 and others); they have no name in the table. Oncogene and tumour suppressor labels come from pan-cancer curation and do not transfer automatically to a cerebellar tumour of childhood; the WNT feedback genes are the proof.
