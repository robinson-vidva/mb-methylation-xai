1. Pathways¶

A single gene rarely explains a tumour. Pathway enrichment asks whether the concordant genes of a subgroup cluster in a known biological process more often than chance. g:Profiler was queried with the concordant genes per subgroup against a custom background of all genes that had both measurements, with GO biological process, KEGG and Reactome as sources. Using the tested genes as background is important: otherwise every list of brain tumour genes looks enriched for brain development.

Group 3 is dominated by a photoreceptor program: detection of light stimulus, visual perception, phototransduction, with NRL, RCVRN, AIPL1, GNAT1 and IMPG2 among the genes. A photoreceptor identity driven by the transcription factors NRL and CRX is a known and targetable feature of Group 3 medulloblastoma (Garancher et al. 2018). The analysis recovers it from methylation and expression without being told. WNT returns the KEGG Wnt signalling pathway, the pathway that defines the subgroup. SHH returns cell fate commitment. Group 4 returns no significant term, consistent with Group 4 being the least understood subgroup.

2. From genes to drugs¶

Why methylation matters for therapy: a promoter that is hypomethylated in one subgroup switches a gene on in that subgroup only. If the gene product can be blocked by a drug, the drug would act on those tumours and spare the others. This is the logic behind subgroup-specific therapy. The reverse case, a gene silenced by hypermethylation, can in principle be reactivated by demethylating agents such as decitabine, which are approved in leukaemia.

The Open Targets Platform collects, for every human gene, whether an approved drug or a clinical compound exists against its protein (tractability), the drugs and their highest clinical stage, and an evidence score linking the gene to each disease. Every concordant gene and every curated driver gene was queried through its public API. Genes were placed in four tiers: approved drug, clinical compound, druggable (a binding pocket or a druggable protein family, but no compound yet), or no tractability data.

This is hypothesis generation. A drug against a gene that is on in a subgroup is a lead to read about, not a treatment. Most of the drugs below were developed for other diseases.

Reading the result. Most concordant genes have no drug and no known pocket. The genes that do have drugs are mostly ion channels, phosphodiesterases and extracellular matrix proteins, with drugs developed for heart, nerve or fibrotic disease. Only one concordant gene, ZFHX3 in WNT, has an Open Targets association with medulloblastoma above 0.3. A few leads are worth noting:

  • SYK and EPHA3 are switched on in WNT tumours. Both are kinases with clinical inhibitors (fostamatinib is approved; KB-004 targets EPHA3 in leukaemia and glioblastoma). WNT tumours already have an excellent outcome, so the clinical question there is de-escalation, not new drugs.
  • IGF1 is switched on in Group 3. IGF signalling has clinical antibodies (xentuzumab) and is a known growth signal in medulloblastoma models.
  • FZD7, FAP, LGALS1 and COL1A2 are switched on in SHH. FZD7 is a Wnt receptor with a phase 1 antibody. FAP, LGALS1 and collagens mark the tumour stroma and the SHH subgroup is known to carry a desmoplastic stroma. These are markers of the microenvironment, not of the tumour cell.
  • MAPK10 (JNK3), switched on in SHH, has clinical JNK inhibitors.

The curated driver genes tell the complementary story.

Two drivers have approved drugs. SMO, the Hedgehog pathway receptor, is the target of vismodegib and sonidegib, the one subgroup-specific therapy that has reached clinical trials in SHH medulloblastoma. CDK6, amplified in a subset of Group 4, is the target of palbociclib and related inhibitors. Neither driver is concordant: their promoters do not change. The two layers of the analysis therefore point at different things. Mutated and amplified drivers are where approved drugs act today. Methylation-controlled genes mark the lineage program and the microenvironment of each subgroup. They are candidates for biomarkers and for drug repurposing, and, for Group 3, they recover the photoreceptor dependency that is already under preclinical study.
